About Hemzo — est. 2024, Ahmedabad

Blood goes bad before it goes anywhere.

India collects enough blood. It just sits in the wrong fridge, in the wrong district, on the wrong day. Hemzo is a forecasting layer for the shelf life nobody watches.

Four rules we
refuse to bend

Operating principles
01
Public data, always
Every forecast is traceable to an open feed. If we can't show the source row, we don't publish the number.
→
02
Shelf life over stock count
A full fridge on day 33 is a shortage in waiting. We rank units by days remaining, never by volume alone.
→
03
Banks decide, we don't
Hemzo sends a signal and a reason. Transfers, consent and custody stay entirely with the licensed bank.
→
04
No donor data, ever
We model units, districts and dates. Not people. There is no donor identity in any Hemzo system.
→
How we got here

Two winters of spreadsheets, one hackathon, and a blood bank in Rajkot that let us read their fridge log every morning.

Mar 2024
One fridge, one spreadsheet
A manual expiry tracker for a single Rajkot bank predicted its first avoidable discard four days out.
Nov 2024
eRaktKosh, parsed nightly
The public feed became a standardized district table — the spine every forecast still runs on.
Jul 2025
First seven-day forecast
Twelve banks in Gujarat started receiving surplus and shortfall signals a week ahead of demand.
Today
1,206 fridges, four states
Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh, with alerts landing before the morning round.
